# Bridge review

Each bridge was checked against the Berean Standard Bible text, the OpenBible.info cross-references and the way the New Testament uses the Old (written with AI assistance; `data/review.json`, generated report `scripts/review_report.py`). The verdicts are suggestions for you to decide on; the app only shows the strength, kind of link and note.

Terms: **quoted** = the New Testament cites the words; **echoed** = a clear allusion (a hint at the words or story without quoting); **shared theme** = only the idea is shared. **Typology** = an earlier person, event or institution that foreshadows Christ. **Votes** = the strongest OpenBible.info reader-voted cross-reference between the two passages.

6 reconsider · 37 revise · 41 keep (the seven appointed-times bridges, p78-p84, were corrected after their review).

## Reconsider: the link is weak or the claim overstates it (6)

### p15 · Daniel's Seventy Weeks Timeline to Messiah the Prince
Daniel 9:24-26 → Mark 1:15; Luke 19:41-44 · listed as *Direct Prophecy* · review: weak link, shared theme, prophecy · 8 votes

Neither Mark 1:15 nor Luke 19:41-44 quotes or names Daniel 9; they share only the ideas of an appointed time and Jerusalem's coming destruction. The 483-year count is a later Christian calculation whose result depends on which Persian decree and which length of year one chooses, and many scholars read Daniel 9 as describing events of the 2nd century BC. The NT's clear use of this part of Daniel is Jesus' warning about the abomination of desolation, a sacrilege in the temple (Matthew 24:15; Mark 13:14), which draws on Daniel 9:27 and related verses.

- neither Mark 1:15 nor Luke 19:41-44 cites Daniel 9; they share only the ideas of an appointed time and the city's destruction
- the NT's clear use of this part of Daniel is Jesus' warning about the abomination of desolation (Matthew 24:15; Mark 13:14), drawing on 9:27
- the 483-year count is a later calculation with competing start dates and year lengths, presented as fact
- many scholars read Daniel 9:24-27 as about the 2nd-century BC crisis; 'the Messiah, the Prince' can also be rendered 'an anointed one, a prince'
- 'Direct Prophecy' overstates a link the NT never makes
