## Our Blessed Hope 01

*2:20:14 · 63 steps · read from KJV (14), NLT (14), KJV, NLT (1)*

**Question:** Is the 'blessed hope' of Titus 2:13 a real, pre-tribulation Rapture, distinct from Christ's second coming, and where is it taught in Scripture?

After a long introduction to his approach (Scripture over opinion, Hebrew and Greek behind the KJV, no organised church), the teacher opens Titus 2:13 and names the 'blessed hope' as the Rapture, separate from the second coming. He works verse by verse through 1 Thessalonians 4:13-18, shows that 'caught up' is the Greek harpazo (rendered in Jerome's Latin as a form of raptus, the source of 'rapture'), and argues it must come before the seven-year tribulation. He then gathers support from Enoch in Genesis 5, the change in 1 Corinthians 15, the 'coats of skins' in Genesis 3 and Psalm 116:15. He ends in John 11, which he reads as Jesus himself acting out a 'mini skit' of the Rapture in raising Lazarus, with Luke 16 showing where Lazarus was while dead, and sets Matthew 24 for the next session.

**What he concludes**

- The teacher holds that the 'glorious appearing' of Titus 2:13 is the Rapture, an appearing in the air that is distinct from the second coming, and that the verse calls Jesus 'the great God'.
- The teacher holds that the word 'rapture' is biblical in substance: 'caught up' in 1 Thessalonians 4:17 is the Greek harpazo, to snatch forcefully out of harm's way, which Jerome rendered in Latin and from which 'rapture' comes; Paul taught it long before John Darby.
- The teacher holds that the Rapture must come before the seven-year tribulation, because believers are not appointed to wrath, Christ as bridegroom would not put his bride through it, and Paul says to comfort one another with these words.
- The teacher holds that at death a believer's body 'sleeps' while soul and spirit go at once to be with the Lord (no soul sleep), and that at the Rapture Christ brings them back, raises and glorifies their bodies, then changes living believers in a moment.
- The teacher holds that Jesus himself taught the Rapture in John 11: 'I am the resurrection' speaks of the dead in Christ and 'and the life' of believers alive on that day, and calling Lazarus out by name pictures how the dead will be called up.
- The teacher holds that Luke 16 is not a parable but a real view of Sheol (the abode of the dead), where Lazarus was in Abraham's bosom before Jesus called him back.

**Topics:** rapture, blessed hope, pre-tribulation rapture, harpazo, 1 Thessalonians 4, resurrection of the dead, glorified body, raising of Lazarus, rich man and Lazarus, seven-year tribulation

## Our Blessed Hope 02 – We Are The Final Generation

*3:45:00 · 103 steps · read from KJV (44), KJV, NLT (1), NLT (1)*

**Question:** Does Scripture show a pre-tribulation Rapture, and how can we know we are the last generation before Christ returns?

He opens with Titus 2:13 and the scoffers of 2 Peter 3, then returns to Lazarus (John 11) and the Ascension (Acts 1) as two acted-out pictures of the Rapture: one for believers who have died, one for believers still alive. He then walks verse by verse through the Olivet Discourse (Matthew 24, with Mark and Luke alongside), using Greek connecting words to separate its parts. He argues that 'nation against nation' means world war, that the fig tree is Israel reborn in 1948, and that the generation seeing that will see everything fulfilled. He lands on Matthew 24:36-41, John 14:1-3 and Luke 21:36 as Jesus teaching a hidden-date Rapture that rescues believers before the seven-year tribulation, and closes with a call to repent and be baptized in Jesus' name.

**What he concludes**

- The teacher holds that Lazarus (John 11) and the Ascension (Acts 1:9-11) are acted-out previews of the Rapture: Lazarus for believers who have died, the Ascension for believers who are alive when it happens.
- The teacher holds that 'nation shall rise against nation' (Matthew 24:7) is a Hebrew idiom for world war, so the end times began with the World Wars, while ordinary 'wars and rumours of wars' (24:6) mark the whole church age.
- The teacher holds that the fig tree of Matthew 24:32 is the nation of Israel, so the generation that saw Israel reborn on 14 May 1948 will see all these things fulfilled, which makes ours the final generation.
- The teacher holds that the Greek 'de' at Matthew 24:36 changes the subject, so 'that day and hour' which no one knows is the Rapture, not the Second Coming, whose date will be knowable once the tribulation starts.
- The teacher holds that the one 'taken' in Matthew 24:40-41 is received as a companion (paralambano, the same word as John 14:3), so being taken is the believer's rescue, and Luke 21:36 promises an escape before the tribulation.
- The teacher holds that God is one person, not a Trinity: the 'Son of God' is Christ's humanity, which is why the Son did not know the day and hour before his glorification.

**Topics:** pre-tribulation rapture, Olivet Discourse, Lazarus as a picture of the resurrection, Ascension as a picture of the Rapture, parable of the fig tree, Israel 1948, last generation, days of Noah, Greek word study, oneness of God

## Our Blessed Hope 03 – How Do We Know We Are The Final Generation

*1:43:53 · 57 steps · read from NLT (9), KJV (5), other (2)*

**Question:** How can we know that ours is the final generation before Jesus returns for His church?

Part three of the series starts from the 'blessed hope' of Titus 2:13 and an outline of Matthew 24. The teacher traces the fig tree as a picture of Israel, from the parable of the fruitless fig tree in Luke 13 to the cursing of the fig tree near Bethphage in Matthew 21 and the 'lesson from the fig tree' in Matthew 24. He then argues that two calculations land on 1948: the Genesis genealogies put Abram's birth in year 1948 after Adam, and Ezekiel 4's 430 years, less 70 served in Babylon, multiplied sevenfold under Leviticus 26 and counted in 360-day years, run from 537 BC to 14 May 1948. Between the two he reads Zechariah 12 as the nations' hostility to the reborn Israel, and he closes by holding that the generation alive since 1948 will see the pre-tribulation rapture.

**What he concludes**

- The teacher holds that the fig tree in Jesus' teaching stands for the nation of Israel, and that its budding is the sign of the last generation.
- The teacher holds that the parable of the fruitless fig tree (Luke 13) pictures Jesus' three years of rejected ministry, and that the cursing of the fig tree near Bethphage acts it out. In his view the tree was 'cut down' when Jerusalem fell in AD 70.
- The teacher holds that the ages in Genesis 5, 7 and 11 add up to Abram being born in year 1948 after Adam, which parallels Israel's rebirth in AD 1948.
- The teacher holds that Ezekiel 4's 430 years, less 70 years in Babylon and multiplied sevenfold under Leviticus 26, give 2,520 prophetic years of 360 days. Counted from 23 July 537 BC, he says these end exactly on 14 May 1948.
- The teacher holds that the generation alive since 1948 will see the pre-tribulation rapture, followed by the Antichrist, the seven-year tribulation and the second coming.
- The teacher holds that 'all the trees' in Luke 21:29 means the other nations, and that their hostility to Israel fulfils Zechariah 12.

## Our Blessed Hope 04 – Daniel's 70 Weeks Prophecy

*39:02 · 23 steps · read from NLT (9), KJV (4), unknown (2), NLT, KJV (1)*

**Question:** How does Daniel's 70 weeks prophecy fix the very day the Messiah would present himself, and what does it say about the end times?

Continuing the 'blessed hope' series from Titus 2:13, the teacher starts from Jeremiah's 70 years of captivity and Daniel's prayer of repentance, then reads Gabriel's answer in Daniel 9 in the NLT and KJV. He argues that each 'week' is a set of seven years, so 69 weeks equal 483 prophetic years of 360 days (173,880 days). He walks through the decrees in Ezra and Nehemiah, holds that only Artaxerxes' decree of March 14, 445 BC concerned rebuilding the city, and counts forward to Palm Sunday, April 6, pointing to Sir Robert Anderson's 'The Coming Prince' for the calculation; this leads him to a Thursday crucifixion. **What he concludes**

- The teacher holds that each 'week' (Hebrew shabua, a set of seven) in Daniel 9 means seven years, so 69 weeks are 483 years.
- The teacher holds that Bible prophecy uses a 360-day year, which Daniel and Revelation confirm by treating 3.5 years as 42 months and 1,260 days.
- The teacher holds that the count starts from Artaxerxes' decree to rebuild the city (Nehemiah 2, March 14, 445 BC), not from the earlier decrees about the temple.
- The teacher holds that 173,880 days from that decree land exactly on Palm Sunday, when Jesus presented himself as Messiah, and that Jesus was crucified on a Thursday, not a Friday.
- The teacher holds that the 70th week is the still-future seven-year tribulation, preceded by the church age and a pre-tribulation rapture, and followed by Christ's millennial reign.

**Topics:** Daniel's 70 weeks, Messiah's timing, 360-day prophetic year, decree to rebuild Jerusalem, Palm Sunday, Thursday crucifixion, suffering servant, seven-year tribulation, pre-tribulation rapture, eschatology

## What Must We Do? Biblical Salvation – Part 1

*2:29:29 · 91 steps · read from NLT (33), KJV (6), NLT, KJV (1)*

**Question:** What must a person do to be saved, according to Jesus and his apostles?

The teacher sets out to answer the biblical question "what must we do to be saved?" He first spends a long stretch on the identity of God, arguing that one God came in the flesh as Jesus. He then argues from Scripture that nothing may be added to or taken from God's word, and attacks the sinner's prayer, infant sprinkling and Trinitarian baptism as man-made traditions. In Luke 24, Acts 1, John 1 and John 3 he builds toward his view that salvation means obeying the gospel by repenting, being baptized in Jesus's name for remission of sins, and receiving the Holy Spirit, and he stops after John 3:21, leaving the rest for Part 2.

**What he concludes**

- The teacher holds that salvation requires obeying the gospel, not belief alone, yet is not earned by works.
- The teacher holds that there is one God, not a Trinity: the Father is the invisible Spirit dwelling in Jesus, whose humanity is the Son of God.
- The teacher holds that Jesus spoke of the Father in figures of speech to distinguish his two natures, human and divine.
- The teacher holds that the sinner's prayer, inviting Jesus into one's heart, and infant sprinkling are man-made traditions with no biblical example.
- The teacher holds that the apostles in Acts give the authoritative plan: repentance, water baptism in Jesus's name for remission of sins, and the gift of the Holy Ghost.
- The teacher holds that the thief on the cross cannot be used against baptism, since the command came after the resurrection.

## Who Do Men Say That I Am? – The Godhead Study

*2:57:09 · 104 steps · read from NLT (40), KJV (39), KJV, NLT (3), NLT, KJV (1)*

**Question:** Who is God according to Scripture, and how are the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit related to Jesus?

Starting from Peter's confession in Matthew 16, the teacher traces the God who created the world, called Abraham and spoke to Moses as "I AM," and argues from Jude, Hosea and Jesus' "I am" sayings in John 8 that this God was Jesus. A long run of Isaiah's "I alone am God" passages supports his case against the Trinity, which he calls a later, non-biblical teaching. He then covers the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit in turn. He argues that the Father is God's spirit, the Son is the human body born of Mary, and the Holy Spirit is Christ's own spirit living in believers. He concludes that the one name in Matthew 28:19 is Jesus, which he promises to develop in a study on baptism.

**What he concludes**

- The teacher holds that the God who created everything, spoke to Abraham and gave Moses the name "I AM" is Jesus before he took on flesh.
- The teacher holds that God is one person, not three, and that the Trinity is a third- and fourth-century departure from the apostles' teaching.
- The teacher holds that the Son of God is Jesus' human body, born of Mary and begotten with a beginning, while the Father is the divine spirit living in that body.
- The teacher holds that Jesus' talk of the Father and the Son is parable (veiled speech, per John 16:25) separating his human and divine natures, not a conversation between persons.
- The teacher holds that the Holy Spirit is the spirit of Christ, the Comforter or Wonderful Counsellor of Isaiah 9:6, living in believers.
- The teacher holds that the single name of the Father, Son and Holy Ghost in Matthew 28:19 is Jesus, and that believing Jesus is God in the flesh is needed for salvation.

**Topics:** Godhead, oneness of God, Jesus is God, I AM sayings, the Trinity, dual nature of Christ, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, the logos, baptism in Jesus' name, Holy Spirit as spirit of Christ

## The Passion Week Of Jesus Christ

*4:58:10 · 172 steps · read from KJV (72)*

**Question:** Why did Jesus choose this week to present himself and die, and how do the days of Passion Week line up with Scripture?

The teacher counts back from 'six days before the Passover' in John 12:1 to date each day of Passion Week by the Hebrew month Abib, using the Bible's sunset-to-sunset day. He walks through the Gospels day by day, from Palm Sunday through the temple clearings, the fig tree, the Olivet discourse, the Last Supper and the crucifixion, and pairs each day with the matching day of creation in Genesis 1. He argues that Jesus was crucified on Thursday, the 14th of Abib, which gives the three days and three nights of the sign of Jonah. Along the way he argues at length for the oneness of God against the Trinity and for baptism in Jesus' name.

**What he concludes**

- The teacher holds that John 12:1 fixes Saturday as the 9th of Abib, so Palm Sunday is the 10th and the preparation day (the 14th) is Thursday.
- The teacher holds that Jesus was crucified and buried on Thursday. Counting 'today' (Luke 23:43) as day one gives exactly three days and three nights, while Friday and Wednesday views do not fit.
- The teacher holds that each day of Passion Week mirrors the same day of creation: light, division, land and plants, signs in the heavens, sea creatures (Jonah), and man in a garden.
- The teacher holds that Jesus ate his Passover a day early because he died as the Passover Lamb when the lambs were killed, and that two Sabbaths followed back to back.
- The teacher holds that Jesus is the one God, the Father, come in the flesh rather than a second person, and that baptism must be done in Jesus' name for the remission of sins.

**Topics:** Passion Week, Passover, three days and three nights, Thursday crucifixion, sign of Jonah, creation week parallel, Hebrew day reckoning, Oneness of God, baptism in Jesus' name, preparation day
